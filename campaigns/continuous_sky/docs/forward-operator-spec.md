# Forward operator spec (revised 6 Nov 2026)

## Main track (6 Nov): one learned field operator for everything that is not sky

Everything between the sky and the counts is one learned operator, not a chain of hand-shaped factors: camera, electronics, optics, transmission and (later) atmosphere. These were previously the WCS polynomial, PSF table, background polynomial and gain.

The lineage is multi-frame super-resolution (Irani & Peleg; Farsiu+; Hirsch, Harmeling+ 2011 for astronomy) and today's multi-view 3D/4D reconstruction:
- BARF / NeRF-- learn camera poses jointly, coarse to fine.
- SCNeRF learns intrinsics and distortion as fields.
- NeRF-W uses a code per image and a transient head.
- Dynamic NeRF adds time.

### Model

For exposure i and detector pixel p:

μ_i(p) = N_θ( ∫∫ K_θ(u, λ | p, m_i, z_i) · s(x⁰_i(p) + u, λ) du dλ ; L-context, p, m_i, z_i ) + B_θ(p | m_i, z_i)

σ_i(p) = σ_pipe(p) · exp h_θ(·)

Transient head: a per-pixel outlier probability π_θ(·) in a likelihood mixture.

- **K_θ.** A kernel field generated by a hypernetwork from (p, m_i, z_i, λ), linear in the sky.
  - Its centroid is the registration and distortion.
  - Its shape is the optics, pixel response and charge spreading.
  - Its sum is the transmission × gain.
  - Its λ dependence is the filter and colour.
  - None of these is a separate model.
- **x⁰_i(p).** The header-WCS position, used only as an anchor and input, like noisy initial camera poses.
- **m_i.** Metadata inputs: instrument token, detector, filter (through its curve), epoch, exposure time, roll (from the header) and readout direction.
- **z_i.** A small learned code per exposure.
- **N_θ.** A nonlinear response with neighbourhood context: saturation, brighter-fatter, IPC. Its low-signal gauge is slope 1.
- **B_θ.** Background, low capacity.
- **The sky s.** Learnable and shared by all instruments: a grid, splats or a field at a declared resolution.

### Rendering

- A deterministic multi-resolution stencil over u (no Monte Carlo; Q2e showed it fails): dense around the core, sparse in the wings out to about 3″, and wide enough to cover the registration range.
- The sky is read at x⁰ + u by B-spline interpolation.
- Each optimisation step uses a minibatch of pixels, like NeRF ray batches.
- Coarse to fine (BARF-style): wide, smooth kernels and low sky frequencies first, then sharpen. This replaces the grid-scan coarse shift.

### Gauges (declared; no data diversity breaks them)

| Gauge | Convention |
|---|---|
| Common blur g (sky ⊗ g, K ⊗ g⁻¹) | sky at a declared reference resolution, e.g. the sharpest kernel's second moment fixed or a declared target |
| Common shift | mean kernel centroid over exposures = 0 at the anchor |
| Common flux scale | mean log kernel sum over a reference instrument = header photometric calibration |
| Smooth sky vs mean background | background per exposure; mean background over exposures of a field = 0 at low order |

### Broken by diversity, tested by data splits

- **Sources of diversity:** dithers, rolls, instruments, epochs, exposure times and fields.
- **Inductive bias:** amortisation (one θ for all exposures, small z_i).
- **Physics:** only as inputs, never as constraints or warm starts.
- **Gate:** tests T1–T7 of `claude/psf-geometry-audit.md` (addendum).

### Baseline

The factorised model below (v33–v36: WCS polynomial, PSF table per detector and band, polynomial background and gain) is the baseline the field operator must beat on T1–T7. The IRAC fully learned instrument (`learned_instrument.py`, frozen sky) is the first prototype of K_θ and N_θ.

### Order

1. Synthetic identifiability: known sky and operator, 2–3 instruments with dithers and rolls, gauges declared.
2. JWST only.
3. JWST + WFC3/IR on the E2 split.
4. Union tiles.

## Factorised baseline (previous spec, 5 Nov)

## Model

For exposure i, detector pixel p = (x, y):

d_i(p) = N_i( e_i(p) ; t_exp, history, epoch, z_i ) + B_i(p) + ε_i(p)

where:

- e_i(p) = A_i[s](p) = ∫∫∫ K_i(p | α, δ, λ) s(α, δ, λ, t_i) dα dδ dλ is the ideal electron image (rate × t_exp × gain).
- K_i is linear in the sky s but **not a convolution**: it varies with p, λ, epoch and exposure.
- N_i is the nonlinear detector response, applied in the detector frame.
- B_i is the background.
- ε_i is noise from a learned noise model.

Linearity in s is the only structural assumption (incoherent optics), and it is what lets one sky be shared by every instrument.

## A_i: linear, shift-variant optics

A_i factors into a filter, a geometry, a PSF and a pixel response:

K_i(p | u, λ) = T_b(λ) · |J_i(p)| · P_i( J_i(p)⁻¹ (u − T_i(p)) ; λ, p, z_i ) ⊗ Π_i(p)

1. **Filter.** T_b(λ) is the band throughput (SVO), integrated by quadrature nodes as now.
2. **Geometry.** T_i(p) is a continuous map from detector pixel to sky: header WCS (all distortion terms), then a learned static correction per detector, then a learned low-order term per exposure. J_i is its local Jacobian (rotation, scale and shear, including the telescope roll).
3. **PSF.** P is a learned field in **detector coordinates** (offset in detector pixels or λ/D units), conditioned on detector position, λ, epoch and the exposure code z_i.
   - Parametrisation: a Gaussian mixture (analytic core and wings) plus a neural residual field (spikes, ghosts) on a bounded window. The mixture is wide enough that nothing is cut at a hard window.
   - Gauge: unit flux and zero centroid per (λ, p); positions live only in T_i.
   - The sky frame sees P rotated and scaled by J_i, so roll changes between visits are handled exactly.
4. **Pixel response.** Π_i(p) is the box integral over the physical pixel in detector coordinates (exact for undersampled pixels such as WFC3/IR 0.128″ and IRAC 1.2″). It is multiplied by a learned intra-pixel sensitivity map (low order) and a per-pixel area and flat residual (a static map per detector, near 1).

**Rendering**, continuous sky × continuous operator, no intermediate sky grid:

- **Splat sky:** a Gaussian splat through a mixture PSF and a pixel box has a closed form (erf products), exact for the mixture part. The residual-field part uses deterministic quadrature on a local fine grid around the pixel (the v21 renderer, generalised).
- **Grid or B-spline sky:** quadrature over the PSF core plus a coarse grid for the wings (the v21 renderer with the kernel taken from P, in the detector frame).
- **Cost:** one evaluation of P per (exposure group, λ node, detector tile), cached; P varies slowly over p.

## N_i: learned detector, physics only as warm start (option 3)

N_i is one generic learned network per instrument, from the ideal electron image to expected counts. It is shared by every exposure and field of that instrument, with a small code z_i per exposure.

- **Inputs:** e in the detector frame, plus metadata: exposure time, epoch, detector, readout direction as a coordinate channel, time since the previous exposure, and earlier frames on the same detector.
- **Architecture:** local 2-D context plus long-range context along columns, and a residual path to identity.
- **No physics in the form.** Known models (pipeline linearity, published charge-transfer and brighter-fatter models) serve only as warm starts, and the network is free to depart from them.
- **Constraints, generic not physical:**
  - N(e) → gain · e at low signal;
  - weights shared across exposures and fields;
  - capacity grows only while held-out likelihood improves;
  - charge conservation as an optional soft penalty.
- **Diversity it needs to be identifiable:** exposure times, dithers, roll angles (the readout direction rotates on the sky), epochs, and other instruments seeing the same sky.
- **First target:** WFC3/IR and ACS, after the A0/A1 runs report.

## Fully learned instrument (separate track)

A single learned operator F_θ from the continuous sky to expected counts and variance, without the A/N factorisation. It sees:
- the sky sampled on a local patch around each pixel's starting position from the header WCS, at the band's quadrature wavelengths;
- the exposure metadata;
- an instrument token and an exposure code.

The only physics kept is that the sky is shared, plus the low-signal gauge (linear response to faint sky).

It is tested first on an instrument with no structured model yet: **Spitzer IRAC**.
- The sky is frozen from the JWST 3D fit (NIRCam F356W/F444W overlap IRAC 3.6/4.5 µm).
- The IRAC operator is learned from 3486 frames and scored on held-out AORs.
- The baseline is a structured operator (detector-frame learned PSF plus per-frame gain and offset).
- If the learned instrument wins on held-out frames and on injection–recovery, IRAC can then inform the sky outside JWST coverage, and the same track is tried on GALEX and SPHEREx.

## Background B_i

A smooth field per exposure plus a static pattern per detector, as now. Low capacity on purpose.

## Solve

- A is linear in s, so the sky step is linear for fixed N, T and P. Each outer iteration linearises N around the current prediction (Gauss–Newton/IRLS; N's Jacobian is local). The sky solve is then conjugate gradients (workstream C).
- Global parameters (P, T corrections, N, noise) take gradient steps summed over tiles; the sky is local per tile (union design).

## Identifiability tests (required before claims)

1. **Injection–recovery** through the physical PSF into raw exposures: point sources, discs and arcs, diffuse light, plus bright saturating sources (tests N1 and N2).
2. **Same sky, several operators.** Adding an instrument must not worsen another's held-out prediction. This is the phase 2 gate that failed with the sky-frame PSF.
3. **Roll test.** Score held-out HST per roll angle. With a detector-frame P the scores must not depend on roll.
4. **Charge-transfer test.** Trail residuals behind bright sources along the readout direction must vanish in held-out ACS/UVIS exposures from different epochs.

## Phasing

| Phase | Build | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | Roll-aware PSF in the current explicit pipeline: the basis kernels defined in the detector frame and rotated per exposure group by J_i (one convolution per (band, roll) group) | JWST + WFC3/IR: NIRCam held-out no worse than JWST-only (2.30); WFC3/IR better than 10.4 |
| A1 | Pixel box and intra-pixel response in the detector frame (undersampled IR) | WFC3/IR S/N>5 and flux error improve; no NIRCam loss |
| A2 | Continuous operator: P as a Gaussian mixture plus residual field, closed-form splat rendering, deterministic quadrature for grids | matches A0/A1 scores, then beats them on injection–recovery resolution |
| N | Learned detector network per instrument (warm start from known models), WFC3/IR and ACS first | held-out bright and saturated pixels improve; injected bright and faint sources recovered |
| L | Fully learned instrument on Spitzer IRAC with the JWST sky frozen | beats the structured IRAC baseline on held-out AORs |

# score_paired: paired scores and residual power by scale (13 Nov 2026)

Files: `score_paired.py` (put it next to `score.py`; it imports it), `run_score_paired.sh` (not launched), `tests/` (`test_score_paired.py`, `fetch_test_data.sh`, `out/` with the real-sample tables, figures and `test_results.json`).

**What the numbers mean.** *Paired difference* = chi2/N(method) minus chi2/N(reference), both over exactly the same pixels of the same exposures (negative = method better). It is bootstrapped over dither groups with the same resamples score.py uses, so the interval is for the difference, not for each score. *Pixel-wise form* = mean over pixels of ((d-m1-b1)^2-(d-m2-b2)^2)/sigma^2; it equals the pooled difference when pixel sets match (checked, to 1e-10) and also gives the share of pixels where the method wins. *Residual power by scale*: r=(d-m-b)/sigma on the scored cut-out, masked pixels 0, power in octave bands of wavelength (pixels), normalised so white noise of unit variance = 1 in every band. (i) **auto**: power of one method's residual; it contains data noise (floor 1 if sigma is right and noise is white) plus model error, so only excess over the floor, or differences between methods, mean something. (ii) **cross** of two methods' residuals on one exposure: the noise is common to both, so it still contains the full noise; it shows only error common to both (or the noise floor) and cannot show errors that differ. (iii) **diff**: power of (model2-model1)/sigma: no data noise, shows at which scales the methods disagree, but not which one is right, and is blind to errors both share. Exact identity diff = auto1 + auto2 - 2 cross (verified to 1e-13). The planned clean measure (cross-power of two different held-out exposures of one filter on the sky) is NOT done: it needs resampling and astrometry.

**Approximations.** Pixels of a pair = those claimed by both methods (none dropped in the sample); each method keeps its own scorer offset. Bootstrap resamples whole dither groups only (pixels independent within, as score.py). byscale cut-out = bounding rectangle of each box (box is rotated, ~55% of the rectangle lies outside it); weight w = scored pixels x cosine taper over 10% of sqrt(box area) inside the box edge; band power = mean of |FFT(w r)|^2/sum(w^2) (the mask-power correction: exact only for a flat spectrum). It is poor (a) for red spectra (bright-source errors): window leakage moves power between scales, (b) for random holes: the test line at 20 px keeps its band but its peak is 32% low with 30% holes (the rest leaks over all bands), (c) at wavelengths above ~1/4 of the cut-out size (2 to 4 cycles, few modes: a 60 px line lands one band too high), (d) the 8 modes of the largest band are noisy. All S/N pixels are used (`--snr-min` to change), common to all methods. Exposures pooled with weight sum(w^2); bands with <4 modes in a cut-out dropped. Short and long channels both in pixels (0.031 and 0.063 arcsec).

**Tests (all pass, `python3 -I tests/test_score_paired.py`, 1.5 min).** (1) 12 real held-out exposures, 2 methods: per-exposure sums per S/N bin for both offset variants are bit-identical to `score.exposure_rows`; headline chi2/N and its 68% interval equal `score.score_run` (fieldop_fix_200 1.960 [1.852, 2.057]; stage3_blot 1.910 [1.754, 2.053]). (2) Synthetic: a sine of amplitude 0.5 gives paired diff 0.125 (expected A^2/2 = 0.125; got 0.124-0.127, interval excludes 0) and a difference-power peak in the right band at 6 and 20 px (peak within 1% of the analytic value at 6 px, 3% low at 20 px); identical models: exactly 0 with interval [0, 0]; white noise: flat power 1 within 4 sigma in every band, with or without 30% random holes; cross-power of identical models also 1. (3) On the real sample the paired 68% interval has width 0.171, against 0.205 (method alone), 0.299 (reference alone) and 0.363 (quadrature sum, what comparing the two separate intervals implies): 2.1 times narrower.

**Real result, SMALL SAMPLE ONLY (12 of 24 held-out exposures, 12 dither groups, 2 per filter; not the full comparison).** fieldop_fix_200 minus stage3_blot, S/N>=5, with offset: **+0.050 [-0.039, +0.133] (68%), [-0.145, +0.200] (95%), P(better) 0.29**: no difference resolved. By S/N bin: <1: +0.044; 1-5: +0.114 [0.061, 0.163]; 5-20: +0.251 [0.204, 0.282]; 20-50: +0.255 [0.234, 0.269]; 50-200: -1.78 [-2.49, -1.22]; >200: +2.5 [-4.7, +10.4]. So on this sample stage3_blot is better at S/N 1-50 and fieldop_fix_200 better at S/N 50-200; the headline is a cancellation. Power by scale (all, 12 cut-outs; fieldop_fix_200 / stage3_blot / diff): 2-4 px 1.48/1.35/0.53; 8-16 px 2.9/2.6/2.2; 16-32 px 4.1/4.6/6.0; 32-64 px 7.5/9.1/9.0; 64-128 px 28/20/18. The two methods differ little at 2-8 px and disagree most at 16-64 px; at 64+ px fieldop_fix_200 is worse in the long channel, better in the short.

**Data caveat.** The `_cal` files named in the splits file are not readable from this machine (404; `vls` shows only `smacs0723_raw` under torchsky-data), so the SCI arrays come from the public MAST `_cal` files (first 20 MB of each). They differ slightly from the protocol's data (ERR differs by about 1%, offsets by up to 0.005; same pixel counts), so the sample numbers above are close to, not equal to, an official run. Test 1 compares both codes on the same data, so it is exact.

**Commands (run in the job environment; copy `score_paired.py` to `protocol/v1/code/` first):**
`FIELD=SMACS SPLIT=dither METHODS="fieldop_fix_200 fieldop stage3_blot" REF=stage3_blot ./run_score_paired.sh`
`FIELD=LMC SPLIT=dither METHODS="fieldop_tiles_fix_sky002 stage3_blot" REF=stage3_blot ./run_score_paired.sh`
(output: `protocol/v1/scores_paired/<FIELD>/paired_*.{json,md}`, `byscale_*.{json,md}`, `byscale_{auto,cross,channel}.png`; set CMD=paired or CMD=byscale for one part). Direct: `python score_paired.py paired --field SMACS --split dither --methods fieldop_fix_200 fieldop stage3_blot --reference stage3_blot`, and `byscale` with `--methods`.

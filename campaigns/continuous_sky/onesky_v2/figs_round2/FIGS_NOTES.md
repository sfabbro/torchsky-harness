# Figures, grid sky versus item sky (SMACS J0723, JWST NIRCam), 13 Nov 2026

Run names (for the record only): grid sky = `states/grid004_state.pt` (0.04 arcsec grid); item sky = `states/items004b_state.pt` (60,000 Gaussian items, raster 0.04 arcsec). Reference fit `fieldop_fix_200_state.pt` and `items002p_state.pt` were not plotted. Script: `make_figs.py` (uses `prep.py` and `kern.py`; band images and kernel profiles are cached in `cache/`; temporary item-state copies with dummy `A`/`pre` keys were written to the scratchpad, `states/` untouched). Console output of the last run: `run.log`.

**Orientation (all figures).** Mosaic header: PC1_1 < 0, PC2_2 > 0, so column increases towards west, row towards north; the sky grid uses the same convention (X west, Y north, grid centre at the box centre). Both are drawn with north up and west to the right. I checked this numerically (correlation of the sky with the mosaic resampled to the sky grid, central 16 arcsec, F150W and F444W): as is 0.98 (item sky) and 0.56 to 0.63 (grid sky, noisier and with a higher background); flipping X, Y, both, or transposing is worse in every case (0.2 to 0.84). By eye the same galaxies, the lensed arc at upper right and the star at lower left line up. No flip applied. No residual shift (the earlier report's 5 to 30 mas offset) was removed.

**sky_three_ways_F150W.png, sky_three_ways_F444W.png.** Official mosaic (`kernel_check/dl/cut_<FILTER>.fits`), grid sky and item sky, band image = Phi_band[f] . coefficients (`flux_ratio.sky_coeffs`), in MJy/sr. Top row: central 20 arcsec box; bottom row: 6 arcsec box centred on the brightest galaxy (X = -0.155, Y = -1.302 arcsec). Asinh stretch (matplotlib AsinhNorm), identical for the three panels in a row; limits come from the mosaic only: 1st and 99.9th percentile of the mosaic in the box for the top row (F150W 0.013 to 6.1, F444W 0.0035 to 3.9 MJy/sr), 1st and 99.5th percentile for the zoom row (F150W 0.105 to 4.2, F444W 0.058 to 2.7); linear width = mosaic 90th percentile minus lower limit. The limits differ between rows and filters, so colours should not be compared across rows. Pixels are drawn at native pitch (mosaic 0.031 arcsec for F150W, 0.063 for F444W, sky grids 0.04), nearest-neighbour, nothing interpolated. Surprising: the grid sky shows a high, noisy diffuse background (about 0.2 to 0.3 MJy/sr in the stretch for F444W against about 0.02 in the mosaic) and, in the F150W zoom, a few dark dots at pixels below the lower limit (negative or near-zero coefficients) inside the brightest galaxy. The item sky is smooth and looks like the mosaic, with some faint star-like specks (F444W star at lower left shows a ring of items).

**sky_ratio_profiles.png.** Brightest galaxy: peak of the 0.4 arcsec smoothed F200W mosaic within the central 14 arcsec; the same peak is found within 0.06 arcsec in all six mosaics, so one centre (X = -0.155, Y = -1.302) is used for every filter and image. Each image (mosaic, grid sky, item sky) is bilinearly interpolated onto a 0.01 arcsec grid (approximation: for the 0.063 arcsec mosaics at F277W to F444W this is crude inside about 0.15 arcsec), background = median of the 2 to 3 arcsec annulus of that image, enclosed flux = sum of (value - background) times cell area for cells within radius r (40 radii, 0.1 to 2 arcsec, cell-centre inclusion, no partial cells); curves divided by the mosaic's. Neighbouring galaxies inside 3 arcsec (the field is crowded; a companion at about 1.3 arcsec south) are included in the aperture and annulus alike. Numbers (grid / item) at 1 arcsec: F090W 3.16/1.08, F150W 2.43/1.07, F200W 2.33/1.08, F277W 2.69/1.17, F356W 3.26/1.17, F444W 3.35/1.16; at 2 arcsec 2.2 to 3.1 / 1.05 to 1.14. At 0.1 arcsec the item sky is 0.72 to 0.97 of the mosaic (sharper than the mosaic, or the mosaic's resampling blur), the grid sky 1.6 to 3.1. The item sky exceeds the mosaic by 5 to 17 percent beyond 0.3 arcsec, rising towards long wavelength; the ratio is not exactly 1. Annulus backgrounds (MJy/sr, mosaic / grid / item) are in `run.log`; the item sky's is lower than the mosaic's in every filter. Small bump near 1.1 arcsec in every curve is the companion galaxy.

**kernel_profiles.png, kernel_encircled.png.** Learned kernel of each fit, loaded with `kc_common.Fit` and measured with `q1_kernel.measure(full=False)` at the footprint centre of the detector with most exposures per filter (short-wavelength B1, long-wavelength BLONG), mean code (no per-exposure term), polar grid (0.0001 arcsec steps to 0.06, then log steps to 10 arcsec, 720 angles). Profile = azimuthal mean about the kernel origin of the package-normalised density (per arcsec^2); encircled fraction = cumulative trapezoid of 2 pi r profile (my integration, from the profile, not the json values; it reaches 0.988 to 1.0 at 10 arcsec because the package normalises on its own level quadrature). Triangles on the axis mark the published widths. Normalisation is the package's (approximation F7 of the earlier report). Grid sky: encircled fraction within 2 arcsec 0.39 to 0.51, about 0.4 to 0.5 of the kernel beyond 2 arcsec in all filters, profiles nearly identical across filters and a core spike near 0.01 arcsec (below the 0.04 arcsec sky pitch), a flat shoulder and a flat floor out to 8.5 arcsec. Item sky: 0.90 to 0.97 inside 2 arcsec; the short filters (F090W to F200W) and long filters (F277W to F444W) form two groups; the core broadens with wavelength (peak density 250 falling to 50 per arcsec^2 from F090W to F444W) and the fraction inside 0.1 arcsec falls from 0.81 to 0.62. Surprising: the item-sky profiles are not monotonic, with a ring-like maximum near 0.02 arcsec and a dip towards the centre (the kernel is peaked off the origin, as for the grid sky, whose peak is 0.004 to 0.009 arcsec off); F277W, F356W and F444W nearly coincide in encircled fraction beyond 0.15 arcsec; the item kernel's width at half maximum was not measured here (the profile peak sits well below the published widths for short filters, as in the earlier report), so "core widens with wavelength" is read from the profile shape and encircled fraction only. A 3 to 5 percent drop at 9 to 10 arcsec is the kernel taper.

**item_population.png.** From `items004b`: `skies[0]["lw"][0]` (two log-widths per item), clamped at log 0.32, w = sqrt(0.016^2 + exp(2 lw)); larger and smaller of the two per item; axis ratio = smaller/larger (so it ranges 0 to 1; the item orientation angle is not used). Map: positions `pos` (arcsec; X plotted to the right as west, as in the sky frame) of the 20,000 items with the largest total coefficient, taken as the sum over the 12 spectral coefficients of exp(lc) (my reading of "total coefficient"; it is not total flux, which would also involve the widths). These 20,000 hold 96 percent of the summed coefficient. Colour = larger width, log scale. Larger width 5/50/95 percent: 0.021/0.100/0.320 arcsec; smaller 0.017/0.058/0.320; axis ratio median 0.86. Surprising: 15,970 items (27 percent) have their larger width at the cap and 8,238 have both widths at the cap (the histogram's spike at 0.32 and the axis ratio spike at 1), so the cap is binding for a large share of items, mostly in the diffuse cluster light and outside galaxies; 541 smaller widths are within 1 percent of the floor. Narrow spikes in the width histograms near 0.06 and 0.11 arcsec look like the initial lattice sizes surviving (not checked against the package). Item centres only exist inside about +-11.3 arcsec (the data region), though the grid is 24.5 arcsec wide; the stars and the lensed arcs are picked out by small, elongated items.

## Build A: zero-level refresh (`--sky-zero masked`)
Changed: `train.py` `sky_zero_refresh`, `_lattice_slices`; `cli.py` option `--sky-zero-px` (default 20000; `--sky-zero-pixels` still accepted; 0 = all pixels).
* Per exposure a fixed pseudo-random subset of render patches is rendered, drawn without replacement with a generator seeded by (`--seed`, a constant, the exposure cut), so the same patches at every refresh.
  Patch side = the render patch of the held-out prediction (at least 32 pixels), or `--sky-zero-patch`. About N / side^2 patches, at least 4. An exposure with at most N pixels is rendered whole, which is the
  earlier full evaluation exactly (and then the smoothing runs over the whole image).
* Smoothing of the model signal (Gaussian, `--sky-zero-smooth`) and the threshold act inside each rendered patch, as a normalised convolution over valid pixels only: at a patch edge a pixel has fewer neighbours,
  so its smoothed signal is noisier and the source mask is slightly narrower there (a source just outside the patch cannot widen the mask inside it). Cost: a small bias toward keeping pixels next to unrendered sources.
* Several tiles: one level per exposure from the source-free pixels of ALL its cuts in all tiles (each tile's sky made active in turn; the budget N is shared between the cuts in proportion to their pixels).
  The "single tile only" stop is gone (tested by the earlier build on a tile set: not re-tested with real several-tile data here).
* Log per refresh: `sky zero (masked): n exposures re-referenced, m fell back to their earlier level, pixels rendered, ..., seconds`. A fallback = fewer than max(100, 5%) source-free pixels. Also in `rec["sky_zero_summary"]`.
* Check 1 (levels, enlarged truth field: 200 x 200 pixel cuts = 40,000 px, 8 training exposures, `tests/check_sky_zero_cost.py 200 8`; the 44-pixel truth cuts have only 1,936 px, so the default renders them whole):

  Seconds per refresh before to after on that field: 52.3 to 21.6 at 20000; to 3.5 at 3,000 px. Time is about proportional to pixels rendered; 20000 on a 4,200,000-pixel exposure would be about 0.5% of the pixels.

## Build B: catalogue-free coarse registration (`--coarse-reg {off,xcorr}`)
Changed: `coarse_reg.py` `coarse_register` (rewritten image step), `coarse_shift`, `xcorr_peak`; `cli.py` `--coarse-reg-radius` (default 1.0 arcsec), `--coarse-reg-inst` (`--coarse-reg-instruments` still accepted; default every instrument except the `--gauge-ref-inst` reference; `all` allowed); call in `cli.main` before `register_prepass`.
* Starting image: `data.splat_coadd` of the reference instrument's training exposures only, on the sky grid. CHOICE: the coadd of the nearest reference band (pivot wavelength, ln scale) is resampled bilinearly at the header positions of each selected exposure's pixels
  (no kernel rendering, no colour model, no blurring to the exposure's own resolution; valid where the coadd has weight at all four neighbours). Scale factors do not matter to correlation.
* Method: both images high-passed (masked block median, asinh stretch, small Gaussian), masked normalised cross-correlation by FFT over integer lags within the radius, parabola sub-pixel peak (after Padfield, as the stand-alone `ref_xcorr/register_xcorr.py`; its upsampled-FFT sub-pixel step was replaced by the parabola). Translation only.
* Output per exposure (log and `reg["coarse"]`): shift in mas, peak height, ratio to the second local maximum (farther than 0.1 arcsec). Refused (translation left at its starting value, normally zero) if peak < 0.05, ratio < 1.5 (the two thresholds of the stand-alone tool: below 0.05 a correlation is noise level for these image sizes; a second maximum within a factor 1.5 of the first makes the choice ambiguous), or the peak lies within 1 pixel of the radius. All refused are listed in one WARNING line.
* The shifts are written to the exposure's starting translation (the quantity `--shifts-from` fills). NEW: the mean translation of the training exposures is then removed from every exposure including validation and held-out ones.
  Without this, the pre-pass removes the mean from the training rows only, and a held-out exposure was left in the old frame (off by the mean, 70 mas in the test), which worsened its score from 2.3 to 16.5 (B instrument) in a first run.
* Check 1, synthetic two-instrument field (`tests/synth_coarse_reg.py`, instrument B pixel 0.12 arcsec, A 0.08), injected translations on B only, drawn between 0.1 and 0.6 arcsec (this draw: 0.27 to 0.48; five B exposures, all selected):

### Build 2: a filter with no training exposure
Run: `--train-exclude-filters F1,F2` (default empty; `cli.py`; `data.py` `load_field`): drops the training and validation exposures of those filters at load time and keeps their held-out exposures. Stops with a message if a name is not in the selected exposures, if an excluded filter would have no held-out exposure left (nothing to predict), or if every filter is excluded. Logs how many exposures were dropped. The excluded filters are listed in `info["train_excluded_filters"]`. Not run on real files (only the synthetic version, `make_field(train_exclude=...)`).

What was checked, what was found, what was done (`model.unseen` = filters with no training exposure, set in `FieldOperator.__init__`; `[]` in every ordinary field):
1. Starting sky (`cli.py` `make_sky`, `train.py` `register_prepass`, `coarse_reg.py`, `sky.py` `spectral_init`): BROKE. The coadd band of the empty filter is all zeros and `spectral_init` treated it as data, pulling the starting spectrum towards zero at that band. FIXED: `spectral_init(..., bands=)` uses only the bands that have data (the smooth prior across the spectral coefficients and the other bands set the empty band). Same for the positivity-margin start. Test: with the empty band as zeros the start reached 0.6 or less of the true band flux; with `bands` it is within 25% (`test_starting_sky_uses_only_bands_with_data`).
2. Band noise estimate of the starting sky (`make_sky`, `sky.sig_b`): the empty band got a meaningless tiny value (used for strata and preconditioning, also in the held-out stage). FIXED: median of the bands with data.
3. Throughput convention (`model.py` `gauge_mean`): the reference set (training exposures of the reference instrument) does not cover the unseen filter's nodes, so the convention `m` there was 0 or noise, not continuous in wavelength. FIXED: for nodes of an unseen filter `m` takes the value of the nearest node (in wavelength) of the filters with training data. `gauge_report` leaves those nodes out of the "mean log throughput is zero" check and lists the filters without training exposures.
4. Blur gauge reference (`model.py`, `blur_ref`): the sharpest filter was chosen among all filters including the unseen one, whose kernel comes only from the warm start. FIXED: chosen among filters with training exposures (identical when all have them).
5. Noise scales per (detector, filter): no fault. The parameter gets no gradient, stays at 0, so the scale is exactly 1. `bound_report` (during and at end of the fit) now skips pairs of unseen filters, so they cannot be read as "at bound"; the end-of-fit audit (`train.py`, `noise_scale_exp_h`) lists them under `noise_scale_not_fitted_no_training_exposure`.
6. Validation carve-out (`data.py`): no fault; a filter with no training exposure has no dither group to draw from and the random stream of the others is unchanged.
7. Zero-level groups (`tiles.py` `pedestal_groups`, `project_gauges`, `sky_zero_refresh`): built from training exposures only, so there is no group for the unseen filter; no fault. `background_mean_per_filter` and the kernel-wing report already skip filters without training exposures.
8. Field normalisations (throughput, distortion): centred and scaled over the (detector, node) pairs observed in training only (`obs_idx`); the unseen filter's nodes are extrapolated by the same functions, consistent with the convention. No change.
9. Kernel: with `--filter-input curve` (default) the unseen filter's code comes from its curve, plus the warm start, which uses the filter's diameter and wavelength (inputs only, no data). With `--filter-input token` the unseen filter's embedding is random and untrained: do not use it for this test (stated in the help text of the option; not guarded).
10. `--pixel-gain detector-filter` and `--filter-shift learned`: an unseen (detector, filter) pair gets an empty map or the starting shift (zero), both neutral by the code; not run.
11. Held-out stage: fits the exposure's own background constant, affine (translation and linear terms), gain `u`, kernel code and distortion code, stripes. The background constant is additive: it cannot supply band flux over the sources (it can absorb a mean level). The gain `u` multiplies the whole exposure but has the tight prior, so it moves the amplitude only a little. The amplitude of the band therefore comes from the sky's spectrum and the learned throughput at that wavelength, which is what the test measures; the background constant can still absorb an error in the mean sky level of the band, so the score is partly a statement about source light and not about the zero level.
